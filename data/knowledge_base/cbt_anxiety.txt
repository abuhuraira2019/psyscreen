CBT FOR ANXIETY: COMPREHENSIVE TREATMENT PROTOCOLS
Based on: Clark & Beck (2010), Craske & Barlow (2006),
CCI Anxiety Workbooks, VA/DoD CPG for GAD Management
===================================================

SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING ANXIETY IN CBT FRAMEWORK
===================================================
Anxiety is the emotional, cognitive, and physiological response to perceived threat. In CBT, anxiety is understood through:

THE ANXIETY CYCLE:
Trigger (internal or external) → Threat Appraisal (overestimate danger) → Anxiety Response → Avoidance/Safety Behaviors → Short-term relief → Long-term maintenance of anxiety

THE THREE SYSTEMS MODEL:
1. COGNITIVE: Worried thoughts, catastrophic predictions, danger overestimation
2. PHYSIOLOGICAL: Racing heart, sweating, muscle tension, shortness of breath
3. BEHAVIORAL: Avoidance, escape, safety behaviors, reassurance seeking

ANXIETY VS. NORMAL WORRY:
Normal worry: Proportionate, time-limited, leads to problem-solving action.
Pathological anxiety: Disproportionate, persistent, uncontrollable, causing significant distress.

SECTION 2: PSYCHOEDUCATION FOR ANXIETY
========================================
Key messages to convey to patients:
1. "Anxiety is your body's alarm system - it evolved to protect you from danger."
2. "In anxiety disorders, the alarm fires too often or for non-dangerous situations."
3. "Anxiety feels dangerous but IS NOT dangerous. Your body cannot harm you through anxiety alone."
4. "The physical symptoms (racing heart, breathlessness) are caused by adrenaline, which is temporary."
5. "Avoidance is the MAIN THING that keeps anxiety going. Facing fears reduces anxiety over time."
6. "Anxiety follows a bell curve - it rises, peaks, and naturally decreases if you stay in the situation."

ROMAN URDU PSYCHOEDUCATION:

- "Magar kabhi kabhi yeh alarm ghalt waqt par bajta hai."

- "Parehz karne se ghabrahat aur barhti hai - mushkilaat ka saamna karna zaroori hai."

SECTION 3: COGNITIVE TECHNIQUES FOR ANXIETY
=============================================

DANGER OVERESTIMATION (Primary cognitive error in anxiety):
Anxious people systematically overestimate:
a) The PROBABILITY of a negative event occurring.
b) The SEVERITY/COST of the negative event if it does occur.
c) Their inability to COPE with the event.

CHALLENGING DANGER OVERESTIMATION:
Questions to ask:
1. "What is the actual probability this will happen? (0-100%)"
2. "What evidence do I have that this is likely?"
3. "Have I faced similar situations before? What actually happened?"
4. "Even if the worst happened, could I cope? What would help me cope?"
5. "What would I tell a friend who had this worry?"

WORRY POSTPONEMENT (for Generalized Anxiety Disorder):
1. Identify a specific "Worry Time" daily (e.g., 5:00 PM for 20 minutes).
2. When a worry arises outside this time, write it on a "Worry List."
3. Tell yourself: "I will deal with this at my Worry Time."
4. At Worry Time, review list. Many worries will feel less urgent.
5. For remaining worries: Classify as Real Problem (use problem-solving) or Hypothetical (use cognitive restructuring).

DECATASTROPHIZING TECHNIQUE:
Step 1: Identify the catastrophic prediction.
Step 2: What is the BEST possible outcome?
Step 3: What is the WORST possible outcome?
Step 4: What is the MOST REALISTIC outcome?
Step 5: If the worst happened, what would you do? How would you cope?

SECTION 4: EXPOSURE THERAPY FOR ANXIETY
=========================================
Exposure therapy is the MOST EFFECTIVE evidence-based treatment for anxiety disorders.

PRINCIPLE: Anxiety decreases (habituates) naturally when a person stays in the feared situation without avoidance.

TYPES OF EXPOSURE:
1. In Vivo Exposure: Facing real feared situations directly (e.g., going to a crowded place).
2. Imaginal Exposure: Vividly imagining feared situations in detail.
3. Interoceptive Exposure: Deliberately inducing feared body sensations (e.g., spinning to cause dizziness for panic disorder).

BUILDING AN EXPOSURE HIERARCHY:
Step 1: List all feared situations related to the problem.
Step 2: Rate each situation for anxiety (0-100 SUDS - Subjective Units of Distress Scale).
Step 3: Order from least feared (0-30 SUDS) to most feared (70-100 SUDS).
Step 4: Start exposure with the LEAST frightening situation.
Step 5: Stay in the situation until anxiety reduces by at least 50%.
Step 6: Repeat each step until it causes minimal anxiety, then move to the next.

EXAMPLE EXPOSURE HIERARCHY FOR SOCIAL ANXIETY (Low to High):
Level 1 (20 SUDS): Making eye contact with a stranger.
Level 2 (30 SUDS): Saying hello to a neighbor.
Level 3 (40 SUDS): Asking a shop assistant for help.
Level 4 (55 SUDS): Making a phone call to a business.
Level 5 (65 SUDS): Attending a small family gathering.
Level 6 (75 SUDS): Speaking in a small group meeting.
Level 7 (85 SUDS): Giving a presentation to a class/group.

SAFETY BEHAVIORS TO ELIMINATE:
Safety behaviors are actions taken to prevent feared outcomes. They maintain anxiety by preventing disconfirmation.
Examples: Sitting near exits, avoiding eye contact, speaking very quickly, checking symptoms repeatedly.
Goal: Gradually reduce and eliminate safety behaviors during exposure.

SECTION 5: ANXIETY ABOUT PHYSICAL SYMPTOMS (HEALTH ANXIETY / PANIC)
======================================================================
Anxiety about physical symptoms involves catastrophic misinterpretation of bodily sensations.

COGNITIVE MODEL OF PANIC (Clark, 1986):
Physical sensation (e.g., racing heart) → Catastrophic interpretation ("I'm having a heart attack") → Increased anxiety → Increased physical symptoms → More catastrophic thoughts → PANIC ATTACK

CBT INTERVENTIONS:
1. Psychoeducation about the physical symptoms of anxiety (explain adrenaline response).
2. Test the catastrophic interpretation with evidence.
3. Interoceptive exposure: Deliberately induce the symptom to show it is not dangerous.
4. Behavioral experiment: Have racing heart, stay with it, see that nothing bad happens.
5. Safety behavior elimination: Stop reassurance-seeking, stop checking pulse.

SECTION 6: GAD-7 SCORE-BASED CBT RECOMMENDATIONS
==================================================
GAD-7 Score 0-4 (Minimal): Monitor. Relaxation techniques, mindfulness introduction.
GAD-7 Score 5-9 (Mild): Worry postponement, relaxation, psychoeducation, self-help workbooks.
GAD-7 Score 10-14 (Moderate): Structured CBT (12 sessions), exposure therapy, cognitive restructuring.
GAD-7 Score 15-21 (Severe): Intensive CBT + consider pharmacotherapy evaluation (SSRIs, SNRIs, buspirone).

SECTION 7: COMBINED PHQ-9 AND GAD-7 ELEVATION (Comorbid Depression + Anxiety)
================================================================================
Comorbidity is very common: 50-60% of those with depression also have significant anxiety.

TREATMENT PRIORITY:
1. If PHQ-9 ≥ 10 AND GAD-7 ≥ 10: Address safety concerns first.
2. Typically treat the PRIMARY presenting complaint first.
3. Behavioral Activation often helps BOTH depression and anxiety.
4. Relaxation and mindfulness benefit both conditions.
5. Referral to a professional is strongly recommended for comorbid moderate-severe presentations.
