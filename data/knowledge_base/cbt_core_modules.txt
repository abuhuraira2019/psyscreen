SECTION 1: THE CBT MODEL - THEORETICAL FOUNDATION
==================================================
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is an evidence-based, structured, time-limited psychotherapy. It is founded on the premise that psychological distress arises from dysfunctional patterns of thinking, feeling, and behaving — and that by changing these patterns, emotional well-being can be significantly improved.

THE CBT TRIANGLE (Cognitive Triad):
- THOUGHTS (Cognitions) → influence feelings and behaviors
- FEELINGS (Emotions) → influence thoughts and behaviors  
- BEHAVIORS (Actions) → influence thoughts and feelings
These three elements are interconnected. Changing one element creates positive change in all others.

KEY PRINCIPLES OF CBT:
1. Collaborative Empiricism: Therapist and patient work together as a team.
2. Structured sessions: Each session has an agenda, homework review, and assignments.
3. Problem-focused: CBT targets specific problems and symptoms.
4. Time-limited: Typically 12-20 sessions for depression/anxiety.
5. Skills-based: Patients learn coping skills they can use independently.
6. Psychoeducation: Patient education about the disorder is a core component.

SECTION 2: PSYCHOEDUCATION
===========================
Psychoeducation is always the FIRST step in CBT. Before any technique is taught, the patient must understand:
1. What depression/anxiety is (symptoms, prevalence, causes)
2. How CBT works (the cognitive model)
3. The role of homework between sessions
4. That recovery is possible with treatment

Key psychoeducation points for depression:
- Depression is not a character flaw or weakness.
- Depression affects mood, thoughts, energy, sleep, and appetite.
- Depression creates a "downward spiral": low mood → withdrawal → less pleasure → lower mood.
- Behavioral activation breaks this cycle.

Key psychoeducation points for anxiety:
- Anxiety is a normal human response to perceived threat.
- The "fight or flight" response is biologically hardwired.
- Anxiety becomes problematic when the alarm system fires too often or too intensely.
- Avoidance maintains and strengthens anxiety over time.

SECTION 3: THOUGHT RECORDS (DYSFUNCTIONAL THOUGHT DIARY)
=========================================================
Thought records are the cornerstone CBT tool for identifying and challenging negative automatic thoughts (NATs).

THE 7-COLUMN THOUGHT RECORD:
Column 1 - Situation: What were you doing? Where? When? Who were you with?
Column 2 - Emotions: What emotions did you feel? Rate intensity 0-100%.
Column 3 - Automatic Thoughts: What thoughts went through your mind? What images?
Column 4 - Evidence FOR: What facts support this thought?
Column 5 - Evidence AGAINST: What facts contradict this thought?
Column 6 - Balanced Thought: Write a more balanced, realistic alternative thought.
Column 7 - Outcome: Re-rate emotion intensity. What will you do now?

HOW TO USE THOUGHT RECORDS WITH PATIENTS:
1. Ask: "What was going through your mind just before you started feeling [emotion]?"
2. Help identify the "hot thought" — the most distressing automatic thought.
3. Guide examination of evidence for AND against this thought.
4. Generate a balanced alternative together using Socratic questioning.
5. Re-rate emotion intensity after the exercise.

SECTION 4: COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING
====================================
Cognitive restructuring is the process of identifying, evaluating, and modifying maladaptive thoughts.

THE ABCDE MODEL (Ellis, Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy):
A - Activating Event: The triggering situation
B - Beliefs: The thoughts and beliefs triggered by A
C - Consequences: The emotional and behavioral consequences of B
D - Disputation: Challenging and examining the beliefs in B
E - Effective Alternative: Developing a more balanced, helpful belief

SOCRATIC QUESTIONING TECHNIQUES:
1. "What evidence do you have for that thought?"
2. "What evidence do you have against it?"
3. "What would you say to a friend who had this thought?"
4. "What is the worst that could happen? Could you cope with it?"
5. "What is the best that could happen?"
6. "What is the most realistic outcome?"
7. "What is the effect of believing this thought?"
8. "What would be more helpful to think in this situation?"

ROMAN URDU COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING PROMPTS:
- "Is soch ka koi saboot hai?" = "Is there evidence for this thought?"
- "Agar aapka dost yeh sochta, toh aap kya kehte?" = "If your friend thought this, what would you say?"
- "Kya koi doosri wajah ho sakti hai?" = "Could there be another reason?"

SECTION 5: BEHAVIORAL ACTIVATION (BA)
=======================================
Behavioral Activation is the primary CBT intervention for depression. It is based on the behavioral model that depression causes withdrawal from rewarding activities, which reduces positive reinforcement and worsens depression.

THE BA CYCLE:
Depression → Low motivation → Withdrawal/Avoidance → Less pleasure/reward → More depression

BREAKING THE BA CYCLE:
1. Activity Monitoring: Patient tracks daily activities and mood (0-10) for 1 week.
2. Activity Scheduling: Therapist and patient collaboratively schedule 2-3 enjoyable or meaningful activities per day.
3. Graded Task Assignment: Break large overwhelming tasks into small, manageable steps.
4. Pleasure and Mastery Activities: Schedule both enjoyable activities (pleasure) and activities that give a sense of accomplishment (mastery).
5. Anti-procrastination strategies: Identify and overcome barriers to activity.

TYPES OF REWARDING ACTIVITIES:
- Pleasure: watching a favorite show, calling a friend, eating favorite food, listening to music
- Mastery: cleaning a room, completing a task, exercising, cooking a meal
- Social: spending time with family, joining a group, volunteering
- Physical: walking, exercise, yoga, sports
- Meaningful: prayer, reading Quran, helping others, creative activities

ROMAN URDU BA ENCOURAGEMENT PHRASES:
- "Choti choti koshish bhi bari tabdeeli la sakti hai."
- "Aaj ek kaam zaroor karein, chahe kitna hi chota ho."
- "Khayal rakhna: kaam karne se motivation aata hai, pehle se nahi."

SECTION 6: PROBLEM-SOLVING THERAPY (PST)
==========================================
Problem-solving therapy is a CBT technique that helps patients develop a structured approach to managing life's problems that contribute to depression and anxiety.

THE 6-STEP PROBLEM-SOLVING PROCESS:
Step 1 - Problem Definition: Clearly define the specific problem (not a vague worry).
Step 2 - Goal Setting: What do you want to achieve? What would success look like?
Step 3 - Brainstorm Solutions: Generate as many solutions as possible (no judgment yet).
Step 4 - Evaluate Pros and Cons: Evaluate the feasibility and consequences of each solution.
Step 5 - Choose and Plan: Select the best solution and create a specific action plan.
Step 6 - Review: After implementing, evaluate the outcome. What worked? What to modify?

DISTINGUISHING REAL PROBLEMS FROM HYPOTHETICAL WORRIES:
- Real problem: Something happening NOW that CAN be solved with action.
- Hypothetical worry: "What if..." scenarios about future events that may not happen.
- For real problems: Use problem-solving.
- For hypothetical worries: Use worry postponement and cognitive restructuring.

SECTION 7: RELAXATION TECHNIQUES
==================================
Relaxation techniques are used in CBT as coping strategies for anxiety, stress, and physical tension.

DIAPHRAGMATIC BREATHING (4-7-8 Technique):
1. Inhale slowly through the nose for 4 counts.
2. Hold the breath for 7 counts.
3. Exhale slowly through the mouth for 8 counts (make a whooshing sound).
4. Repeat 4 times.
Benefits: Activates the parasympathetic nervous system; reduces physiological anxiety response.

PROGRESSIVE MUSCLE RELAXATION (PMR):
PMR involves systematically tensing and then relaxing muscle groups throughout the body.
Sequence: Start from feet, move upward: feet → calves → thighs → abdomen → hands → arms → shoulders → neck → face.
For each muscle group: Tense for 5 seconds → Release → Notice difference for 30 seconds.

GROUNDING TECHNIQUE (5-4-3-2-1):
For acute anxiety or panic, use sensory grounding:
- 5 things you can SEE
- 4 things you can TOUCH/FEEL
- 3 things you can HEAR
- 2 things you can SMELL
- 1 thing you can TASTE
This technique brings attention to the present moment and reduces catastrophic thinking.

SECTION 8: MINDFULNESS-BASED CBT (MBCT)
=========================================
Mindfulness is the practice of paying attention to the present moment, non-judgmentally.

CORE MINDFULNESS PRACTICES:
1. Mindful Breathing: Focus attention solely on the breath. When mind wanders, gently return.
2. Body Scan: Systematically bring awareness to each part of the body from toes to head.
3. Mindful Observation: Observe thoughts as "events" in the mind, not facts.
4. Mindful Walking: Focus on each step, the sensation of feet on the ground.

MBCT PRINCIPLE - DECENTERING:
Patients learn to observe negative thoughts with distance: "I am having the thought that I am worthless" rather than "I am worthless." This "decentering" or "defusion" reduces the emotional impact of negative thoughts.

MINDFULNESS FOR ROMAN URDU/URDU SPEAKERS:
- "Is waqt mein rehna seekhein" = Learn to stay in the present moment.
- "Soch ko soch ki tarah dekhein, haqiqat ki tarah nahi" = See thoughts as thoughts, not reality.
- "Saans ko mehsoos karein" = Feel your breath.
