CBT FOR DEPRESSION: COMPREHENSIVE TREATMENT PROTOCOLS

CCI Depression Workbook Modules

SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING DEPRESSION IN CBT FRAMEWORK
======================================================
Depression in CBT is understood through the "Negative Cognitive Triad" (Aaron Beck, 1967):
1. Negative view of SELF: "I am worthless, inadequate, a failure."
2. Negative view of the WORLD: "Everything is unfair, the world is against me."
3. Negative view of the FUTURE: "Nothing will ever get better. There is no hope."

These three patterns of negative thinking maintain and deepen depression. CBT directly targets all three.

COMMON NEGATIVE AUTOMATIC THOUGHTS IN DEPRESSION:
- "I can't do anything right."
- "No one cares about me."
- "I'm a burden to everyone."
- "Things will never improve."
- "I don't deserve to be happy."
- "I'm too tired to do anything."
- "What's the point of trying?"

ROMAN URDU NEGATIVE AUTOMATIC THOUGHTS IN DEPRESSION:
- "Main kisi kaam ka nahi hoon."
- "Koi mujhe pasand nahi karta."
- "Main sabke liye bojh hoon."
- "Sab theek nahi hoga kabhi."
- "Koshish karne ka kya faida."

SECTION 2: BEHAVIORAL ACTIVATION FOR DEPRESSION - DETAILED PROTOCOL
=====================================================================
Behavioral Activation (BA) is considered the MOST EFFECTIVE behavioral intervention for depression.

PHASE 1 - ACTIVITY MONITORING (Week 1):
Patient tracks every activity for one full week using an Activity Diary:
- What they did each hour
- Mood rating (0-10, where 0=worst mood, 10=best mood)
- Pleasure rating (0-10)
- Mastery/accomplishment rating (0-10)

Analysis questions after week 1:
- What activities were associated with better mood?
- What times of day were mood best/worst?
- How much time was spent in bed or avoiding activities?

PHASE 2 - ACTIVITY SCHEDULING (Weeks 2-4):
Create a weekly activity schedule with:
- 2-3 pleasurable activities per day (things you used to enjoy or might enjoy)
- 1-2 mastery activities per day (tasks that give sense of achievement)
- Gradual increase in social interactions
- Regular sleep and meal times (routine is crucial)

IMPORTANT: Schedule activities based on PLAN, not mood. In depression, motivation follows action, not the reverse.

GRADED TASK ASSIGNMENT:
For tasks that feel overwhelming, break them into the smallest possible steps.
Example - "Clean the house" (overwhelming) becomes:
Step 1: Stand up and go to the kitchen (2 minutes)
Step 2: Clear one small section of the counter (5 minutes)
Step 3: Wash 3 dishes (5 minutes)
Step 4: Rest and acknowledge the achievement.

ROMAN URDU GUIDANCE FOR BEHAVIORAL ACTIVATION:
- "Uthiye aur ek chota kaam karein. Mehnat ke baad achha lagta hai."
- "Aaj ek purana shauq dobara try karein."
- "Roz ek baar ghar se bahar zaroor niklein, chahe thodi der ke liye."
- "Namaz, tilawat, aur zikr mood behter karte hain."

SECTION 3: COGNITIVE RESTRUCTURING FOR DEPRESSION
===================================================

IDENTIFYING DEPRESSION-SPECIFIC COGNITIVE DISTORTIONS:

1. ALL-OR-NOTHING THINKING (Black and White Thinking):
Definition: Seeing things in absolute, extreme categories.
Depression example: "I made one mistake at work. I am a complete failure."
Challenge: "Is it truly all-or-nothing? Can one mistake define my entire worth?"
Balanced thought: "I made one mistake. Everyone makes mistakes. I can learn from this and do better."

2. CATASTROPHIZING (Magnification):
Definition: Exaggerating the importance of problems or imperfections.
Depression example: "I forgot to call my friend. She will never speak to me again."
Challenge: "What is the most realistic outcome? Am I exaggerating this?"
Balanced thought: "I forgot to call. I'll call her now and apologize. She will likely understand."

3. OVERGENERALIZATION:
Definition: Drawing sweeping conclusions based on a single event.
Depression example: "I failed this test. I always fail. I'll never succeed in anything."
Challenge: "Does one failure mean I always fail? What evidence contradicts this?"
Balanced thought: "I failed this specific test. That's disappointing, but it doesn't mean I always fail."

4. MENTAL FILTER (Selective Abstraction):
Definition: Focusing exclusively on one negative detail while ignoring the positive.
Depression example: One critical comment ruins an otherwise successful presentation.
Challenge: "Am I ignoring all the positive feedback? What is the full picture?"

5. DISQUALIFYING THE POSITIVE:
Definition: Rejecting positive experiences by insisting they "don't count."
Depression example: "My boss praised my work, but he was just being nice. He didn't really mean it."
Challenge: "What evidence do I have that the positive is not real?"

6. SHOULD STATEMENTS:
Definition: Using "should," "must," "have to" in rigid, demanding ways.
Depression example: "I should always be productive. I must never let others down."
Challenge: "Where did this rule come from? Is it realistic and flexible?"
Balanced thought: "I prefer to be productive, but rest and imperfection are human and acceptable."

7. PERSONALIZATION:
Definition: Taking excessive responsibility for external events.
Depression example: "My friend seems upset. It must be something I did."
Challenge: "Am I taking responsibility for things outside my control? What other explanations exist?"

SECTION 4: BEHAVIORAL EXPERIMENTS FOR DEPRESSION
===================================================
Behavioral experiments test the validity of negative beliefs through real-world action.

EXAMPLE BEHAVIORAL EXPERIMENT:
Negative belief: "If I go to the family gathering, I'll feel worse."
Experiment: Attend for 30 minutes only.
Prediction: I will feel worse (rate current mood: 4/10).
Outcome after experiment: Mood was actually 6/10 during gathering.
Conclusion: The prediction was wrong. Social contact can help, not hurt.

SECTION 5: PHQ-9 SCORE-BASED CBT RECOMMENDATIONS
===================================================
PHQ-9 Score 0-4 (Minimal): Monitor. Provide psychoeducation on mood hygiene, sleep, exercise.
PHQ-9 Score 5-9 (Mild): Behavioral Activation, thought records, self-help CBT workbooks.
PHQ-9 Score 10-14 (Moderate): Structured CBT program (12 sessions), activity scheduling, cognitive restructuring.
PHQ-9 Score 15-19 (Moderately Severe): Combination of psychotherapy + consider pharmacotherapy evaluation.
PHQ-9 Score 20-27 (Severe): Urgent referral to mental health professional. Combined pharmacotherapy + CBT.
